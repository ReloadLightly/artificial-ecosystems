# The EVOLVE episodes

One research program, four numbered models, twenty-nine years.
The invariant is the sentence from 1970: do not impose a fitness
function. Let selection be the bookkeeping of a finite world.
What changes, episode to episode, is how rich that world is allowed
to be.

## 1970 — Conrad & Pattee

*Journal of Theoretical Biology* 28: 393–409.

A one-dimensional loop of places. Conserved chips. Cell-like organisms.
No fitness function. Five qualitative results, four of them rerun here.

This is `src/evolve1970`.

## 1981 — EVOLVE

Light, temperature, three genes, an amenability locus. Evolvability
itself is selectable.

## 1985 — EVOLVE II

Conrad & Strizich. Scheduled abiotic change. In slowly varying worlds,
organisms developed resistance to phenotypic change.

## 1985 — EVOLVE III

Rizki & Conrad. 2-D regions, fifteen traits, producers and decomposers,
critical vs modifier sections of a nucleic-acid genome. A 1992 extension
(O’Callaghan & Conrad) added parasitic feeding.

## 1989 — artificial worlds

Conrad & Rizki name the method. Fitness and population dynamics both
emerge from local interaction in a closed material world.

## 1998–1999 — EVOLVE IV

Brewster & Conrad, *Evolve IV: a metabolically-based artificial
ecosystem model* (LNCS 1447) and the 1999 CEC paper on niche
specialization. Organisms exchange metabolites and modify the local
environment. Niches form. There is no EVOLVE V. Conrad died in 2004.

`src/evolve4` is a first reconstruction of that move: two conserved
metabolites, heritable conversion direction, a food cycle that is
physics rather than a diagram.
